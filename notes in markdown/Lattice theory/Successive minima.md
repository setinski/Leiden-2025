>[!define] #definition
>##### $i$-th minima of a [[Lattice|lattice]]
>Let $\mathcal{L} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be a lattice of rank $k$ and let $i \in \{ 1,\ldots,k \}$. The $i$-th minimum of $\mathcal{L}$ is defined as
>$$\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{L}) \coloneq \inf\{ s > 0 \mid \dim(\mathcal{L} \cap s \mathcal{B}^{n}) \geq i \}.$$

Note that for $i = 1$, $\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L})$ is indeed the length of a shortest non-zero vector of $\mathcal{L}$. It is clear that $\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}) \leq \lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}) \leq  \ldots \leq \lambda_{k}(\mathcal{L})$.

>[!lemma] 
>Let $\mathcal{L} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be a lattice of rank $k$. There exist linearly independent vectors $\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{k} \in \mathcal{L}$ such that $\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\| = \lambda_{i}(\mathcal{L})$ for all $i \in \{ 1,\ldots,k \}$. In particular, every $\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{L})$ is finite.

>##### Proof
>Let $R \coloneq \max_{i = 1}^{k} \| \mathbf{b}_{i} \|$, so that $\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{L}) \leq R$ and hence, if the $\mathbf{y}_{i}$'s we are looking for exist, they must satisfy $\mathbf{y}_{i} \in R \mathcal B \cap \mathcal{L}$ for all $i \in \{ 1,\ldots,k \}$. Define recursively $\mathbf{y}_{i}$ as a shortest vector in $R \mathbf{B} \cap \mathcal{L} \setminus \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{i-1})$, statement which is well-defined by the finiteness of the discrete and bounded set $R \mathbf{B} \cap \mathcal{L}$. Then it is clear that $\dim(\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\| \mathcal B\cap \mathcal{L}) \geq \dim(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{i-1},\mathbf{y}_{i}) = i$, so that $\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\| \geq \lambda_{i}(\mathcal{L})$ for all $i$. To note that the converse inequality holds, observe that by definition of $\mathbf{y}_{i}$, if $\mathbf{y} \in (\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\| - \varepsilon) \mathcal B\cap \mathcal{L}$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$, then $\mathbf{y} \in \operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{i-1})$, i.e. $\dim((\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\| - \varepsilon) \mathcal B\cap \mathcal{L}) \leq i-1$.

>[!info] Note
>The $\mathbf{y}_{i}$'s constructed above may not form a basis of $\mathcal{L}$. For example, if $D_{n} \coloneq 2 \mathbb{Z}^{n} \cup (2 \mathbb{Z}^{n} + (1,1,\ldots,1))$ and $n \geq 2$, then $\lambda_{1}(D_{n}) = \lambda_{2}(D_{n}) = \ldots = \lambda_{n}(D_{n}) = 2$ and the lattice generated by the $\mathbf{y}_{i}$'s is exactly $2 \mathbb{Z}^{n}$.

>[!lemma] 
>For any lattice $\mathcal{L}$ of rank $n$ we have $\prod_{i = 1}^{n} \lambda_{i}^{(2)}(\mathcal{L}) \geq \det \mathcal{L}$.

>##### Proof
>Consider the linearly independent vectors $\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{n}$ as above and note that they generate a full rank lattice $\mathcal{L}' \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, hence $\prod_{i = 1}^{n} \|\mathbf{y}_{i}\|_{2} \geq \det \mathcal{L}' \geq \det \mathcal{L}$ as desired.^[The first inequality follows from $\det \mathcal{L}' = \prod_{i = 1}^{n} \|\mathbf{b}_{i}^{*}\|_{2}$ and $\|\mathbf{b}_{i}\|_{2}^2 = \|\mathbf{b}_{i}^{*} + (\mathbf{b}_{i} - \mathbf{b}_{i}^{*})\|_{2}^2 = \|\mathbf{b}_{i}^{*}\|_{2}^{2} + \|(\mathbf{b}_{i} - \mathbf{b}_{i}^{*})\|_{2}^{2}$.]
