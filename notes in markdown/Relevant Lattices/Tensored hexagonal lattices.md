>[!define] #definition
>##### Hexagonal lattice
>The hexagonal lattice $\mathcal{H}$ is defined as the $2$-dimensional lattice generated by the basis $\mathbf{B}$ made up of the vectors
>$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1
\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{ \frac{3}{4} } \\ \frac{1}{2}
\end{pmatrix}.$$

>[!lemma] 
>The densest sphere packing in dimension $2$ is attained by the hexagonal lattice $\mathcal{H}$, which satisfies $\gamma(\mathcal{H}) = \sqrt{ \frac{4}{3} } (= \gamma_{2})$.

^654bc6

>##### Proof
>Trying to apply once the [[Lagrange Reduction|Lagrange algorithm]] on $\mathbf{B}$ proves that it is Lagrange reduced, hence $\gamma(\mathcal{H}) = \frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H})^{2}}{|\det \mathcal{H}|} = \sqrt{ \frac{4}{3} }$. Let now $\mathcal{L} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{2}$ be any full-rank lattice and let $(\mathbf{b}_{1},\mathbf{b}_{2})$ be a Lagrange-reduced basis of $\mathcal{L}$. Without loss of generality, we may assume $\mathbf{b}_{1} = (0,1)^{T}$, $\mathbf{b}_{2} = (\alpha,\beta)^{T}$ where $|\beta| \leq \frac{1}{2}$. Hence $\gamma(\mathcal{L}) =  \frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{2}}{|\det \mathcal{L}|} = \frac{1}{|\det \mathcal{L}|} = \frac{1}{\alpha}$, and from $1 \leq \alpha^{2} + \beta^{2} \leq \alpha^{2} + \frac{1}{4}$ we deduce $\alpha \geq \sqrt{ \frac{3}{4} }$, which implies the claim.

>[!define] #definition
>##### Tensored Hexagonal lattices
>We define recursively $TH_{i}$ by setting $\mathbf{B}_{0} = (1)$, $\mathbf{B}_{i+1} = \mathbf{B} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{i} = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{i}&\frac{1}{2} \mathbf{B}_{i} \\ 0 & \sqrt{ \frac{3}{4} } \mathbf{B}_{i} \end{pmatrix}$ and $TH_{i} \coloneq \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}_{i})$.
